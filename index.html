About

I am a second-year doctoral student in the Graduate School of Interdisciplinary Information Studies at the University of Tokyo, advised by Prof. Yoshio Ishiguro. My work sits between human–robot interaction and robot perception. The question I keep coming back to is simple: a person sees a place and wants the robot to go there, so why should they have to read a map or drive the robot by hand?

My answer is to let the instruction stay human (a photo, a stroke drawn on a photo) and to make the robot do the hard part: find that instruction in the map it already has, without external sensors, without per-site calibration and without asking the user where they are. I build the systems on real robots with ROS 2, LiDAR and cameras, and I evaluate them in field user studies. Before the doctoral program I worked for two years as a localization algorithm engineer in autonomous driving, on SLAM and multi-sensor fusion.

Research

Point and shoot: a photo as the navigation goal

Under reviewACM/IEEE HRI 2027 · Systems track

The user photographs the place they want the robot to visit. The system finds the matching viewpoint in the robot's map and the robot drives there on its own. No map reading, no joystick, and the user never has to locate themselves. Evaluated against map-click and teleoperation in two field studies on a campus site, eight months apart.

Localizing one narrow-view photo in a prior map

Under reviewIEEE Robotics and Automation Letters

Untrained users take tightly framed, zoomed-in photos, and that is exactly where image retrieval breaks. This work re-ranks the retriever's candidates using the robot's own pose-annotated mapping run as the only supervision, with no manual labels and no real queries. It improves every retriever and dataset we tested and raises cold-start relocalization success on a physical robot.
